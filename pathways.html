Composite Pathways · Research Brief

Education Pathways:
Trafford & Selective London

A school-level composite of how pupils move through selective education in three stages — primary → secondary, secondary → post-16, and post-16 → higher education. It stitches together separate aggregate datasets for different cohorts and years; it is not a single followed group of children.

← Back to the League Tables

⚠️ How to read this

This is a composite, not a tracked cohort

Publicly accessible sources do not allow end-to-end tracking of one named cohort across all three transitions. True longitudinal matching exists only in the DfE’s Longitudinal Education Outcomes system — a restricted dataset accessed via the ONS Secure Research Service. The public material is aggregate-only, so every “pathway” here is a stitched-together school-level composite, with different cohorts and different years under each stage.

London scope note. The named London schools sit in selective clusters, but most London boroughs are comprehensive. Any discussion of “selective destinations” applies only to the named grammar-school pockets — not to Greater London as a whole.

🧭 The three-stage pathway

The stages have very different data availability, so confidence is uneven. Stage 1 must be reconstructed from FOI and admissions material; Stages 2 and 3 are official DfE publications.

STAGE 1

Primary → Secondary

No central school-to-school dataset. Reconstructed from FOI disclosures, admissions policies & LA admissions material — often redacted under section 40(2).
Confidence: medium–low
STAGE 2

Secondary → Post-16

DfE Key stage 4 destination measures, including a school-level institution dataset (sustained education / apprenticeship / employment).
Source confidence: high
STAGE 3

Post-16 → HE / Training

DfE 16-18 destination measures and Progression to higher education or training (degree, top-third HE, level 4+).
Source confidence: high

Source confidence “high” means the official dataset is clearly identified and documented — it does not mean a full school-by-school extraction has been completed. Stage 1 confidence varies by whether a school disclosed counts, disclosed only a feeder list, or refused on data-protection grounds.

🎓 Trafford — Stage 1 reconstruction

The strongest Trafford evidence comes from Sale Grammar School. In its internal-review response for September 2021 entry, Sale said the cohort contained 192 offers (96 male, 96 female) drawn from 45 primary and junior schools. It disclosed gender-by-school counts for 10 schools, total counts for a further set where the school total was ≥3, and withheld the remainder on personal-data grounds — the residual being 24 offers across 20 unnamed schools.

Because Sale explicitly said 24 further offers were spread across 20 unnamed schools, any reconstruction that totals only the named schools will understate the true spread of primary origins. That undercount is itself a finding, not a defect in reporting.

Trafford admissions context

The 2021 admissions material confirms a shared entrance-test arrangement among AGGS, Sale, Stretford and Urmston — one exam sitting, results shareable across those schools. AGGS’ intended admission number for that cycle was recorded as 204. Current material puts Stretford’s PAN at 160. These are admissions context and should not be substituted uncritically for historical 2021 PANs where the relevant admission-year booklet has not been pinned down school by school.

🏙️ Selective London clusters — Stage 1

London evidence is materially thinner than Trafford. The best example is Nonsuch High School for Girls (Sutton), which refused per-primary counts on s.40(2) grounds but did disclose a full list of feeder primaries without counts for the 2022 and 2023 entry years. Nonsuch therefore evidences feeder breadth, not intensity.

Nonsuch — admissions context (2024 NWSSEE & catchment)

For that cycle Nonsuch offered 85 places by rank in the second-stage test irrespective of catchment, 35 places for girls in receipt of pupil premium who passed (also irrespective of catchment), and then up to 125 places by catchment — defined as a 5.25 km radius from the school. These are admissions-policy figures, not feeder-school counts.

Other London clusters — Sutton boys’ grammars, Bexley grammars, Kingston’s Tiffin schools, Barnet’s QE Boys and Henrietta Barnett, and Redbridge’s Ilford County and Woodford County — yielded no equivalent feeder-count tables in this run. Absence of a public school-by-school feeder table is a data gap, not evidence that no feeder pattern exists.

📊 Stage 2 — secondary to post-16 (official)

The official source is the DfE Key stage 4 destination measures. The latest release (2023/24) follows pupils who finished KS4 in 2022/23 and records their destination the year after — the % continuing to a sustained education, apprenticeship or employment destination.

Key stage 4 leavers — institution-level destinations

The right school-level dataset is the institution-level destinations file (43,994 rows in the latest release). After school identifiers, the row variables are:

overalleducationfessf · school sixth formsfc · sixth-form collegeother_eduapprenall_workall_notsustall_unknown

This is exactly the source the app’s Destinations view consumes for secondary schools. What remains open is the full row-by-row extraction of the named schools across the latest and prior two releases — an extraction task, not something to fill by inference.

🎓 Stage 3 — post-16 to HE / training (official)

Stage 3 needs two official DfE publications.

16-18 destination measures

The 2023/24 release covers pupils from state-funded mainstream schools and colleges who left 16-18 study in 2022/23. The institution-level file (75,660 rows) carries:

overalleducationhefeother_eduapprenall_workall_notsustall_unknown

Progression to higher education or training

The institution-level destinations dataset sits at school geography (21,744 rows, time period 2021/22). Its destination fields include:

Note: the DfE changed the top-third HE calculation for the 2016/17 cohort and again for 2020/21, so comparisons over time need care.

🧩 Synthesis — two visible pathways

A Trafford route (relatively high Stage 1 confidence)

The 2021 offer data show Sale Grammar drew especially heavily from Brooklands, Moorlands Junior, Springfield and Park Road Sale, with a second tier including St Mary’s C.E. (Sale), Willows, Bowdon C.E., Tyntesfield and St Joseph’s Catholic. This is a genuine primary-to-secondary feeder pattern — but not a tracked cohort into sixth form or university; the later stages would come from the separate official KS4 and 16-18 datasets.

A Nonsuch route (breadth, not weight)

The 2023 feeder list shows Nonsuch’s intake spans a very large footprint across Sutton and neighbouring boroughs and counties — from local Sutton and Cheam schools to Kingston/Merton feeders and some independent juniors. Because the count breakdown was refused under s.40(2), the list evidences which primaries appeared, not which ones dominated.

✉️ Ready-to-send FOI templates (Stage 1)

Three reusable templates for recovering feeder-school counts. Replace [SCHOOL NAME] and [YEAR].

1 · Academy grammar where you need feeder counts

Dear FOI Officer,

Under the Freedom of Information Act 2000, please provide the school of origin of pupils who were offered Year 7 places for September [YEAR] entry to [SCHOOL NAME].

Please provide the information in the following format:
Primary school name | number of offers

If full counts are considered exempt because of section 40(2), please provide:
 - all rows where the number of offers is 3 or more;
 - a separate line showing the suppressed residual total for all schools below the disclosure threshold; and
 - confirmation of the total number of offers made for that Year 7 intake.

Please confirm whether the list is based on the offer stage or the final September census/on-roll position, and whether independent schools are missing because of CTF or other MIS limitations.

Yours faithfully

2 · School that disclosed a feeder list but not counts

Dear FOI Officer,

Thank you for previously disclosing the names of primary schools which sent successful applicants to [SCHOOL NAME].

I am now requesting a partial-count disclosure for September [YEAR] entry in the following format:
Primary school name | number of offers — for every feeder school where the count is 3 or more, plus:
 - the number of suppressed feeder schools below that threshold;
 - the combined number of offers across those suppressed schools; and
 - the total number of Year 7 offers made.

This request is designed to avoid identification risks while still allowing meaningful aggregate analysis.

Yours faithfully

3 · Local authority admissions team

Dear Admissions Team,

Under the Freedom of Information Act 2000, please provide any aggregated information held by the authority on the primary schools of origin of pupils offered Year 7 places at [SCHOOL NAME] for September [YEAR] entry.

If school-level counts cannot be released in full, please disclose:
 - all feeder schools with 3 or more offers;
 - the combined number of offers across all suppressed schools; and
 - the Year 7 planned admission number and total offers made.

Please state whether the authority holds this at common-offer stage, final allocation stage, or census stage.

Yours faithfully
